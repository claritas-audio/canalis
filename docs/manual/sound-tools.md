# Sound tools

Loudness, spectrum, A/B comparisons, automatic level and recording: the tools on every strip
for hearing and measuring what a chain does.

## Spectrum

**Spectrum · in / out** under a strip's loudness unfolds a live spectrum, from 20 Hz to
20 kHz, with lines at 100 Hz, 1 kHz and 10 kHz:

- What goes into the chain, dim.
- What comes out, in the chain's colour.

It is the way to see what an EQ does. The chain analyzes only while the spectrum is open.

## Comparing two versions

**A/B** at the right of a strip's record row starts a comparison: B is a copy of what plays
now, and you keep editing A.

1. Change a plugin.
2. Click **B** to hear the other version (the chain reloads it), and **A** to come back.
   Changes go to whichever is playing.
3. Click **keep** to end the comparison on the version you are hearing.

The comparison survives closing the window.

## Automatic level

**auto** beside a strip's loudness target turns on automatic level. The chain slowly raises
quiet sources and lowers loud ones toward the target:

- It moves up 1 dB a second and down 4, between +12 and −24 dB.
- It holds still in silence, so it never lifts the noise.
- It backs off fast when peaks near full scale.

While it is on, the button shows the gain it applies, **auto +3.2 dB**. Exported chains keep
the setting.

> [!TIP]
> Automatic level is good on a Desktop EQ chain, so a quiet video and a loud one
> come out even.

## Recording a chain

**● Record** under a chain's output meter writes what the chain outputs (after every slot
and the mute) to `~/Music/Canalis/<name> <n>.wav`, 32-bit float at the graph's rate, until
**■ Stop**. The time runs beside it.

Then the line says where the file went, with **open folder**.

- It records only while something plays through the chain.
- If the disk falls more than two seconds behind, the gap is noted.
